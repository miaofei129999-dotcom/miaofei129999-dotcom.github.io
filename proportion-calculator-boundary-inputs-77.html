Exact to 30 Digits, Wrong at the Eighth

Two numbers were wrong out of seventy-seven, and both of them were in the fine print.

That is the short version of a week I spent trying to break my own calculator and mostly failing. I fed it negative ratios, zero denominators, mixed numbers, a decimal thirty digits long. It handled all of it. Then it printed a zero where the answer was one billionth, and I had to sit with that for a minute.

The setup

The proportion calculator is a static page. Every calculation runs in the browser through one ES module — 264 lines, BigInt fractions, shared with the slope calculator on the other site. No API, no server round trip, nothing to mock.

Seventy-seven inputs, five modes, run under Node 22.22.0. Then I wrote the expected answers a second time in Python using fractions.Fraction, deliberately from the spec rather than from the JavaScript, and compared the two as exact rationals. Floats would have hidden the failures I was hunting.

The two refusals in the first row are their own small story. 1,000 is rejected — no comma in the grammar. A 33-character decimal is rejected too, because the parser stops at 32.

What the two failures have in common

Both are printing problems, and both sit directly under a correct answer that the same page already computed.

1/1000000000 returns c = 1/1000000000 and then adds: decimal preview ≈ 0. Not a rounded billionth. Plain zero.

1000000000000000000000000000000 / 7 returns the exact fraction, then previews 142857142857142850000000000000. The true expansion is 142857142857142857142857142857. Seventeen correct digits, thirteen zeros that belong to nobody, error of roughly seven trillion.

The culprit is two lines at the bottom of the rational class, where the value is turned back into a JavaScript double for display. The arithmetic never goes near a double. Everything above those two lines is exact; everything below them is approximate and labelled as if it were a courtesy.

This is usually the graveyard. Zero denominators are where a calculator divides by zero and prints Infinity into a box a student will copy into homework. Here the page stops and says the question has no answer, which is the mathematically correct thing to say and the harder thing to code.

Negatives, fractions as inputs, mixed numbers as inputs, a decimal point at the front or the back — all fine. The input grammar is more forgiving than the label on the box suggests.

Boundary of this test

Module only. The browser UI, the SVG plot, the draggable points and the copy button were not exercised, so nothing here says the page wires the module up correctly.

No traffic data either. I can't tell you how often a visitor meets those magnitudes, and inventing a frequency would be the same sin as the fake zeros — a number that looks like evidence and isn't.

Eight-decimal rounding is also not the bug. 1/7 previewing as 0.14285714 is correct and the text says "up to 8 decimal places". The bug is that the preview is derived from a float, so at both extremes it stops being a rounding of the right value.

Open thread

The repair is unglamorous. Derive the preview from the numerator and denominator directly, and when the true value is smaller than the smallest digit on offer, print nothing instead of a zero.

Everything else in the module earned my trust this week. The line under the answer has not.
