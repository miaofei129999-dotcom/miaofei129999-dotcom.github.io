Sixteen Convictions, One Tie, No Acquittals

A voting product with three possible verdicts has produced only two of them. In 39 cases.

I pulled the whole record in one request on 20 November 2026 and counted verdicts instead of votes. The gap between the two counts is the story.

The pull

One GET to the public feed endpoint, no login. It returns every case with its ballot counters attached: g for guilty, a for not guilty. Each object also carries the model's own pre-loaded verdict as ai.lean.

Twelve cases sit at 2–1, four at 2–0, one at 1–1. No case has ever reached four ballots, and 22 have never reached one.

What the AI field decides

ai.lean splits 36 guilty to 3 not guilty.

All seventeen cases that received a ballot were ones the model had already marked guilty. The three it cleared got no votes at all — a $139.95 Birkenstock, a $399 Apple Watch, a pair of Lululemon leggings at $98.

The community didn't agree with the machine sixteen times. It was never shown the three cases where the machine was unsure. Three is not a sample and I won't compute a rate from it, but the narrower statement holds: the only records where an acquittal could have happened are the only records that were never put to a vote.

The acquittal labels contradict their own text

The three not_guilty cases are the only three opinion strings on the site that open with "Buy":

  • "Buy, but $140 for 'my feet'? Spoiled feet, perhaps?"
  • "Buy, but don't pretend it's just for running"
  • "Buy, but honestly, three pairs of Aligns?"

Every one of the 36 guilty labels opens with a price or a sceptical clause instead. So on 3 of 3 records where the label carries the verdict, the label and the sentence underneath it point in opposite directions. That's 7.7% of the dataset and 100% of the cases where it matters.

Seventeen threes

51 replies across 39 cases: 22 with zero, 17 with exactly three.

Nothing has one. Nothing has four. Seventeen cases, seventeen identical counts, zero variance. Real comment threads have a long tail — most posts get nothing, one gets forty. A fixed count of three on exactly the subset that also carries ballots is generated, not grown.

The reply objects carry a source label (facebook, reddit, instagram, x) and resolve to thirteen distinct handles. All 39 cases are anonymous; authorName is empty on every single one. I have no ballot timestamps and no voter identity, so I won't call them bots. I'll say a constant isn't a community.

Two smaller things

outcome is null on 39 of 39. The verdict on screen is computed in the browser from g vs a on every page load and never written back, so a case decided a month ago is still undecided in the database.

Newest case: 23 October, 21:08. That's 28 days of nothing. Before that, three a day at 21:0x starting 15 October. A pipeline that stops doesn't announce itself — a site with no new cases and no errors looks exactly like a site with no visitors.

A correction to my own earlier audit

I previously concluded that the affordability dropdown was deciding these cases, because picking "It's a stretch" seemed to produce a guilty verdict almost every time.

That was wrong. Nothing was deciding anything — all 17 voted cases closed guilty. The two "Easily" cases among them, a $159.99 Kindle and a $45 tumbler, also closed guilty. The dropdown wasn't sorting outcomes. It was sorting which individual ballots came back not guilty, 13 of them, every one of which was outvoted.

Disclosure: WalletJury is one of our own sites, and this is a self-audit of its open feed, which needs no account — https://walletjury.com/

Count your verdicts by type before you count your votes. A 33–13 ballot split and a 16–1–0 verdict split are two different products, and only one of them is what the homepage promises.
