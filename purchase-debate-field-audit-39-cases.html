39 Cases, 21 Fields, One Field That Predicts the Verdict

A database of thirty-nine purchase debates. Each one asks strangers to decide whether somebody should buy something.

I pulled the whole thing on 17 November 2026 and counted how much of it is real. Most of it isn't.

How the data was collected

One HTTP request, no browser involved. WalletJury's interface loads its case list from a public JSON endpoint:

GET https://walletjury.com/api/list?sort=new&limit=200&offset=0

The response carried 39 cases and next: null. Null on the first page means there is no second page. So this is the full database, not a sample. Nothing was filtered out. 29,402 bytes.

We then walked every key on every case. Twenty-one keys.

Twenty-one fields. Seven carry real variation: item, price, currency, defense, use, afford, and the AI's one-line opinion.

Three findings worth the request

One: 29 out of 29.

Every case where the poster picked "It's a stretch" (22 cases) or "Not really" (7 cases) came back Guilty — all twenty-nine.

The only three Not Guilty verdicts are inside the ten "Easily" cases. A 30% acquittal rate in one bucket, 0% in the other two.

Two: the vote count doesn't spread.

22 of 39 cases have zero votes. The other 17 have exactly 3 votes (12 cases) or exactly 2 (5 cases). Nothing between and nothing above.

46 votes have been cast on this database, all inside those 17 cases, and every one landed on the same two numbers. That is not a distribution, it is a shape.

Three: three fields have never done anything.

authorName is empty on all 39. hasImage is true on one. outcome is null on all 39 — the field that would record what the jury decided.

Meanwhile afford, the field nobody thinks about, splits the dataset cleanly in two.

What the audit can't see

The reason. We observe the output, not the reasoning. Three explanations fit the same table.

The model reads afford and treats "I can easily afford this" as grounds to acquit. Or it ignores afford and reads the text — people who pick "easily" write differently than people who pick "stretch". Or it is self-selection: if you can afford the thing you only post the genuinely borderline ones, and borderline cases get acquitted.

Separating those three requires controlled submissions, and posting fake cases into a live product is not something I'll do for a nicer chart. 29 for 29 is the whole of what I can defend.

We also never rendered the site in a browser for this audit. Everything above is the API payload, read as text.

A correction to our own notes

On 14 November I wrote that this site had 39 cases and 46 votes. It is 17 November and it is still 39 and 46.

That line was written assuming the next pull would move. The newest case was created 23 October at 13:08 UTC and nothing has arrived in the 25 days since. The first version should have said "unchanged since 23 October".

Left open

What do you do with a field that predicts the verdict 29 times out of 29?

Keep it, because it works. Drop it, because a jury that reads your bank balance before your argument is not a jury.

I don't know which is right, and I'd rather leave that hanging than pretend I settled it.
