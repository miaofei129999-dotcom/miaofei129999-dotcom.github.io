157 Empty NCAP Pages: A Vehicle Safety Coverage Ledger

Status page for our car spec database. Current state: the safety layer is broken for most non-Honda vehicles, and the maintenance layer is empty by design.

Why this page exists

A reader pointed out that the safety page for a 2020 Toyota Camry claims NHTSA never tested the vehicle. NHTSA rated it five stars. We crawled 162 pages to see how far the problem goes.

Method

94 brand sitemaps from carspecdata.com/sitemap.xml → 2,792 URLs of the form /<brand>/<model>/<4 digits>. Two excluded (/ram/1500, /saab/9000 — model names, not years). Stratified sample of three model-years per brand: 170 pages, 61 brands. /maintenance on all 170, /safety on 162. curl via proxy, text parse, 8 November 2026.

Read it this way

The blank maintenance table is intentional. Fluid capacities and torque live in paid service information, and we don't publish a number we can't trace. A wrong torque spec destroys an engine; an empty cell costs a visit to the dealer.

The blank safety table is not intentional. It is broken, and it is broken in a way that correlates with the manufacturer rather than the model year — median year on failing pages was 2021, while the three working pages were 2017, 2026 and 2010.

The explanatory copy on those 157 pages blames model-year age and low production volume. That copy is ours, and it is false for at least three of the vehicles we opened.

Not measured

The importer was not inspected; the cause below the symptom is unconfirmed.

No baseline for NHTSA's own NCAP coverage, 2018–2025. The 157 cannot yet be split into our-misses and never-rated.

Corrections

  • "Older model years / low volume" explanatory text: withdrawn. Replaced with a plain statement that no record matched.
  • Claim that every model year carries a star rating: withdrawn. 3 of 162 in this sample carry one.
