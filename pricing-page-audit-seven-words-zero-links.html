Six Words and No Exit: An Audit of a Pricing Page Nobody Curled

Pricing pages are usually the simplest page on a site. Mine turned out to be the only page on the site with no links in it.

This is the audit. Fourteen URLs, all in the public sitemap, all answered 200, and one of them is a completely different shape from the other thirteen.

What was counted

For each of the 14 sitemap URLs I saved the raw response and measured three things:

  • visible text, after removing <script> and <style> blocks
  • hrefs that point to another page on the same host, excluding CSS, JS, favicon and self-canonical
  • Question nodes in JSON-LD, and whether each question's text also appears in the delivered HTML

The three findings

One: the price is in a meta tag, not in the page. Three tiers at $4.99, $9.99 and $19.99, with a clear description of what a point buys and a promise that points never expire. Beautifully written — in meta description, in og:description, and in the React payload. The body of the document has none of it. There is no Product or Offer schema either; the only structured data on the page is an FAQPage.

Two: eight answered questions exist only for machines. Where the jobs come from. What one point buys. Whether refunds exist. Average answer 36 words. Not one of those eight question strings appears in the served HTML — they are painted after hydration from a single self.__next_f.push call. Sitewide it is 54 Question nodes, 46 backed by visible text, and all 8 orphans on this page.

Three: nothing links out. The only internal references in the document are the CSS file, the JS chunk and the favicon. Every other page carries 9 to 18 page links. A visitor arriving from a search result has no in-product path forward.

Privacy and terms are the next weakest at four links each, which is what a server-rendered nav buys you.

What I got wrong before

Nine days ago I wrote that this page renders four words server-side. Six, measured properly, plus an em dash. The number was wrong in the small way that makes you distrust the note — off by a rounding slip, right about the shape.

The shape: correct page in the browser, empty document on the wire.

Not tested

Googlebot's rendered DOM. Google runs JavaScript, so the eight FAQ answers may well be visible to it. I am reporting the initial response, not the render, and I did not open Search Console in this run.

Bing. No account, no data, no claim.

Revenue. A dead-end pricing page probably costs money. "Probably" is the strongest thing I can say without analytics.
