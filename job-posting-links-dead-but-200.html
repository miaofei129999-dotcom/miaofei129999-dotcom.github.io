Two of Six Job Links Are Dead, and Both Answer 200

A careers page can look completely healthy and be a tombstone. I found two this morning while re-checking six job links that a page about posting lifetimes had published as verifiable examples.

Neither returned an error. Both returned 200.

The six rows, re-opened on 20 November

The page in question is about how long job postings stay up. It lists six roles with first-published dates and says all six still opened on 6 November 2026. It was updated on the 15th.

Both dead links serve full pages with success status. Stripe's 148 KB response contains the words "no longer accepting applications". GitLab's 77 KB response redirects to job-boards.greenhouse.io/gitlab?error=true, which is the board telling you the requisition is gone.

Why a link checker can't see this

I ran one first. Eleven of eleven links came back 200 and I wrote down "citations healthy" before reading a single page body. That was wrong, and it's the whole reason this post exists.

Greenhouse and Stripe don't 404 a closed posting. They serve the shell and swap the content. Every automated outbound-link audit on the market will score these as fine, forever, because none of them read the body.

2,435 roles. 67.3% of the Greenhouse set is more than a month old, against the page's 68%. Different day, different subset, same shape — the "postings last one to two weeks" advice is simply wrong, and that part of the page holds up.

Three things that didn't go the way I expected

The two dead roles were the two youngest. Half a day old and eleven days old when the numbers were pulled. The survivors were 29, 89, 196 and 968 days old. Older did not mean deader; it meant the req was still genuinely open.

A 200 is the normal way a job link dies. Not a 404, not a redirect home, not an empty body. Greenhouse hands back a styled error state, Stripe hands back a full careers page with a notice where the role used to be. Both are correct product decisions and both defeat every automated check.

One role has been listed for 968 days. Engineering Manager, EU at Ashby, first published March 2024. Fifteen of the 79 roles on that board are over 30 days old. A posting's age describes how long a door has been open, and says nothing at all about whether anyone is walking through it.

What I can't tell you

When Stripe's role closed. It was alive on 6 November and dead on 20 November, which brackets it somewhere between day 10 and day 24 after publication. That's a six-day window and narrowing it needs daily polling.

I also can't reproduce the 3,272 total. Postings that closed between 28 October and today are gone from the API, so a surviving-set median is biased downward by construction. Comparing my survivors to their full set and calling the difference a finding would be dishonest arithmetic.

Disclosure: JobRadarly is one of our own sites, and both dead links are still on the page as of publication. Everything above came from its own HTML and from public ATS endpoints — jobradarly.com

Pick six outbound links on your site and read the text on the other side, not the status code. The 200s that say "no longer open" are the ones nobody has looked at.
