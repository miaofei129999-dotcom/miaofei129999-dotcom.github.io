Facebook Public Video Board: One Snapshot, Eight Entries

One board snapshot, listed in order, with the play counts.

Facebook does not run a public "most watched" page the way YouTube does. What it serves to a logged-out visitor is a live board that refreshes continuously, with no queryable endpoint behind it. Below is one snapshot of that board, taken on October 7, 2026 at roughly 09:00, listed in the order it was shown, with the runtime and the play count exactly as displayed.

One pattern is worth stating before the list: seven of the eight entries are not in English.

  • Kitten and cake, 13 seconds, about 120 million plays. The caption is emoji, four hashtags, and no words at all. It is also the only entry above twenty million.
  • 11 seconds, 18.7 million plays, posted two days earlier. Caption is three words in Tagalog.
  • 16 seconds, 18.15 million plays, one day old. Vietnamese caption.
  • A Burmese-language drama clip, 2 minutes 58 seconds, 1.35 million plays. The longest entry on the board, and the first one under 10 million.
  • 58 seconds, 987 thousand plays, Burmese, posted 22 hours earlier. A shared clip resurfacing from an older post.
  • 16 seconds, 777 thousand plays, Burmese. Mid-length watch, still present at both checks.
  • 27 seconds, 617 thousand plays, Burmese. This is the entry that dropped off the board between the two checks.
  • 31 seconds, 14 thousand plays, Vietnamese, posted one hour before the pull. Fresh content, smallest count on the list.

Burmese appears four times, Vietnamese twice, Tagalog once. The single entry with no language barrier at all is the kitten, and it is also the one that travelled furthest. A board described as showing what is popular, in the version served to a visitor who is not logged in, is overwhelmingly content for audiences the platform serves heavily and the English-language press rarely covers.

Two things about the numbers. First, the drop is not gradual: the top three run 11 to 16 seconds and hold roughly 157 million plays between them, while everything from fourth place down runs longer and carries under 1.4 million. There is no smooth curve from the top of this board to the bottom. Second, every entry is fresh. All eight were posted within three days of the pull, and nothing older made the board at all.

The board also moves. Six hours later the play count on the top entry had already shifted, and an entry that was absent in the morning had appeared. That is what this board is: a live window, refreshed continuously, not a weekly or all-time ranking. The counts beside each video are running totals, but the list itself is not stable enough to read as a chart.

Method note: no login, no ad account, no targeting. Play counts are as displayed at the two check times, and they are totals rather than weekly gains.

Snapshots like this go stale within hours, which is the point.
