Sixty-One Words a Card, and No Setting That Says So

The most useful thing about this audit is that it destroyed a number I had been quoting for a week.

We publish flashcard decks built from public-domain books. A reader asked whether the answers are capped, because they all look the same length on screen. I went looking for the cap. There isn't one. What there is, is a sentence limit and a library full of books written in short lines.

Method

Deck pages embed the full deck as window.BC_CARDS. Regex, json.loads, no browser.

217 sitemap URLs → 174 decks → 37,426 cards → 10,050 chapter buckets. Four threads, 67 seconds, one parse failure from an escaped quote in the inline JSON. Words counted by token regex, sentences split with an abbreviation guard.

Prior run for comparison: 13 November, 34,500 cards on 156 decks. The gap is +2,926 cards, +18 decks, +60 sitemap URLs.

Findings

No answer is cut off. 36,949 end with a full stop, 461 with a closing quote, 14 with a question mark, 2 with an exclamation mark. Zero end mid-sentence. The maximum value appears once. If a hard cap existed you would see a stack of cards at that number, all incomplete. There is no stack.

Sentences are what's fixed. 14,722 answers are two sentences, 17,731 are three. That's 86.7%. Length is product, not target.

The short decks are the aphoristic ones. Meditations, Art of War, Prince, Tao Te Ching, Enchiridion — five books built from one-paragraph fragments, five shortest decks, ranked in the same order as their sentence density. Site average is 24.50 words per sentence; Meditations is 16.19.

Deck size doesn't explain it (correlation 0.04). Question length doesn't (0.05). What differs is how the source writes.

Boundary

The payload carries no source text, so I can't separate "the book writes short sentences" from "that deck was configured to write short sentences." Both look identical from outside. And nothing here is a check on accuracy — I did not verify a single answer against its book.

Retraction

On 13 November this site said the answers "sit in a 60-word band, which suggests a 60-word target." That line is wrong and it's off the site. The band is what three sentences of average density produces.

*Disclosure: this is our own site, and the cards are public. The crawl ran on 19 November 2026.*

The assumption I'd held — that uniform output means a uniform constraint — turned out to be the expensive kind of wrong. Uniform output can also mean a uniform structure over varied material.
