Seventeen Links, Fourteen Pages, and a Page Nobody Points At

A site with fourteen pages has seventeen internal links. That is the entire link graph, and it fits on one screen.

I crawled all of it on 17 November 2026. Most of the audit is about the one page that gets none of those links.

Method

Every URL in jobradarly.com/sitemap.xml, fetched one at a time. All fourteen returned 200.

From the saved HTML, every <a href> was extracted by regex, relative paths resolved against the page URL, # anchors and mailto: dropped, and only same-domain targets kept. That left 17 unique internal targets.

Each target was then requested a second time with redirects disabled, so a redirect reports as a redirect rather than resolving to its destination. Any response that was not 200 got two further attempts before being written down — the retry rule that has caught four false dead-link reports for us this year.

Three things that were not what they looked like

One: the 404 belongs to Cloudflare.

/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection shows up on thirteen of fourteen pages because Cloudflare's email obfuscation rewrites each mailto into a scripted link. With JavaScript running, it works. Read as raw HTML, it points at a page that does not exist.

The real count of broken internal links on this site is zero. A checker that says thirteen is describing itself.

The longest page — 2,852 words explaining what the product cannot do, which is the most useful thing a product can publish — is linked from the hub and from no other guide. Zero sibling links. The shortest guide has six.

Nineteen sibling links exist in total. Eleven of the nineteen, 58%, point at two pages.

Three: 54 FAQ questions are marked up for a feature that stopped showing.

Nine pages carry 54 questions in FAQPage markup: 12 on the limits page, 8 on pricing, 5 or 6 on each guide, 3 on about. No duplicates. Somebody wrote all of them by hand.

Since 2023, FAQ rich results have been limited to government and health sites. Fifty-four carefully written questions currently earn zero extra space in a search result.

On the same eight pages, not one Article block declares an image — the property Article markup still gets credit for.

What the audit can't see

Whether this costs anything. I don't have Search Console for this domain open, so I cannot tell you which of the 14 URLs are indexed, what any of them rank for, or whether the link imbalance has moved a single visit.

A JavaScript-rendering crawl was not tested either. Everything above is the raw HTML one request returns.

And the pattern may be deliberate. Keeping the limits page quiet makes commercial sense if it is the least profitable page on the site. That would be a decision rather than a defect, and it isn't mine to reverse.

A correction we owe

In October I wrote that this site had zero JobPosting markup and no job detail URLs, and let that imply it "skips structured data".

Half of that was wrong. Eight of these pages carry three JSON-LD blocks each — Article, BreadcrumbList, FAQPage — with dates matching the sitemap's lastmod to the day. I went looking for a mismatch and there wasn't one.

The JobPosting half still holds. The other half was sloppy, and sloppy lines get quoted back at you.

Left hanging

Four sentences of HTML and ten minutes would fix the inverted links.

Whether to make that change is a different question. A page that honestly lists what your product cannot do is either the most trust-building page you own or the fastest way to lose a signup, and I have exactly one data point — my own opinion.
