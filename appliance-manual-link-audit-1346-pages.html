A Manual Page That Links a Government Dataset Instead of a Manual

Midea has 80 manual pages on my appliance site. Not one of them links the manufacturer.

Summit has 47, also nothing. GE Profile 30, nothing. Beko 25, Asko 23, nothing and nothing.

Meanwhile GE, the parent brand, has 109 manual pages and 109 manufacturer links.

That gap is what sent me through all 1,346 of them.

How

sitemap-models.xml on modelnumberfinder.com lists 6,800 URLs. Each model carries four sub-pages — manual, problems, parts, specs — leaving 1,346 URLs that end in /manual/.

I fetched every one. Five curl workers, 30-second timeout, one retry, a regex over each page's href attributes, hosts recorded. 1,346 pages, 1,346 HTTP 200s.

78 brands across 586 dryers, 404 washing machines, 356 dishwashers.

Three findings

1. A brand string is enough to break the link.

Nine brands get a manufacturer link on all their models: Amana, Bosch, Electrolux, Frigidaire, GE, Kenmore, KitchenAid, LG, Samsung. That is 792 pages, no exceptions.

GE Profile is the same company as GE and points at the same support portal. It has no entry, so its 30 pages fell through to a default branch, and the default branch is a paragraph explaining that no verified link exists — followed by a Department of Energy dataset.

2. The seven PDFs are the visible edge of a manual paste.

Whirlpool has 121 manual pages and 114 manufacturer links. Maytag has 46 and 45. Those gaps sum to 7, and the site contains exactly 7 PDF links: six Whirlpool, one Maytag, all stored on a single S3 bucket.

Seven files imported by hand. The rest generated. No other brand has a PDF.

3. The fallback paragraph is better written than the feature.

Open a Speed Queen dryer page and the title says "Manual — Official". The body says: *We do not have a verified manual link for the Speed Queen ADE6H178T.* Then it explains that Speed Queen serves manuals behind a model lookup rather than at fixed URLs, tells you where the model number sits on the machine, and describes the manual — 24 to 40 pages, one cycle chart for the drum, symptoms in place of error codes.

Honest, specific, useful. Which is exactly why 547 of these pages shipped without anybody flagging them.

Related: 1,344 of 1,346 pages state that the model has no published error-code list. The Bosch error-code page, for the brand with the deepest dishwasher coverage here, prints "0 codes confirmed" above two header rows and no data.

What I did not test

The 792 manufacturer links: I did not verify they resolve to the correct document. They are JavaScript lookups, sometimes captcha'd, sometimes geo-blocked. That is a real test and it is not in this one.

The 7 PDFs: never opened, so I cannot confirm the file matches the model.

The 547 DOE links: host confirmed, contents not followed. A fuel-and-water dataset's usefulness to someone with a dead dryer is a question a link audit cannot answer.

And the site only builds manuals for three appliance types. Refrigerators and ovens have none, so 1,346 is the size of the current build, not of the category.

Disclosure

I built ModelNumberFinder, so the 67 brands with no link are my omission: modelnumberfinder.com. Replacing the allowlist with a per-brand default is a day of work for the nine that function. The 67 that do not — Miele, Viking and others publish manuals behind lookups too — need a different page, and I have not settled on what it should say.
