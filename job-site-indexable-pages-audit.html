14 Indexable Pages, Zero Job Postings

Before the audit: we thought this site had a rendering problem. After the audit: it has 14 pages, and none of them is a job.

That matters more than it sounds. A job aggregator lives or dies on whether a specific opening can be found by a specific person on a specific day. Ours can't be found at all, because no opening has an address.

How this was measured

The sitemap lists 14 URLs. We pulled every one with curl through a single proxy on 9 November 2026 and counted what a crawler would see: visible words after stripping scripts and styles, JSON-LD @type values, and raw occurrences of the string "JobPosting".

Then four route probes and a read of robots.txt and llms.txt.

Three findings

Not one JobPosting node, on a job site. Fourteen pages, zero. Nine carry Question and FAQPage markup. The guides are aimed at queries like H1B sponsorship and new-grad hiring in 2027 — precisely the queries where a search engine would rather return a listing than an essay.

There is no address to index. /job/1 returns 404. /search returns 404. /jobs returns 307. Postings are read from company boards at request time and served behind /api/, which robots.txt disallows. So the crawl ends at page 14, every time.

/pricing is a four-word page. It's listed in the sitemap at priority 0.8 with a monthly change frequency. The server-rendered HTML carries 4 words and no links, plus FAQPage markup describing a Q&A block that isn't present. Someone deciding whether to sign in gets what a crawler gets: almost nothing.

What we can't say

We didn't log in, so the number of live postings behind the API is unknown to us. "Zero indexable jobs" is the claim; "zero jobs" would be a lie.

We didn't check Search Console, so we don't know how these 14 pages are actually treated, or whether the guides pull any traffic at all.

And we have no conversion data from guide to signup.

A correction

An earlier note of ours blamed client-side rendering. That was wrong — the guide pages are plain server-rendered HTML, one to nearly three thousand words, fully readable with JavaScript disabled. The problem was never paint. It was that nobody had built the URL.

Disclosure

This audit covers jobradarly.com, a site of ours. We publish it because the visible product is finished and the crawlable surface is nearly empty, and those two things can coexist for months without anyone noticing.

A schema tag is not the fix. A page is the fix: one posting, one URL, a date it was first seen, the company, the city, canonical back to the employer's board. The markup can follow.

The awkward part is what that does to freshness. A page that survives the posting is a page that lies, and this product exists to avoid exactly that.
